# Workshop Lesson 01 — Algorithm Shifts, AI-Driven Marketing, and Theory of Constraints for Growth

**Session:** Opening lecture ("The Genius Shortcut") of the 2-day Acquisition.com/Mozi scaling workshop, delivered 08-19 by the operating partner of Acquisition.com (former martial-arts/kickboxing gym owner, early Gym Launch client, record-setting Prestige Labs seller, 4 years building sales/marketing functions at Gym Launch, helped scale Prestige Labs to ~$70M lifetime supplement sales, sat on the executive board during Alex and Leila Hormozi's exit prep, "led marketing teams that manage over two thousand individual businesses"). The session sets up the entire two days: it defines "genius thinking," walks the room through the three radical changes in marketing over the last 18 months (interest-based algorithms, Meta's Andromeda update, AI infinite scale), and teaches Theory of Constraints as the diagnostic lens for everything that follows ("we cannot solve anything in business without understanding theory of constraints"). The rest of the workshop agenda he previews: a Founder Hot Seat (live funnel breakdown), a "how to 100x your content and paid ads" session, and a wealth/investments presentation from COO Neil. Format was lecture with heavy live audience polling and call-and-response.

## Lessons & Frameworks

### 1. The room benchmarks (context for where a business "should" be)

The speaker pulled U.S. business revenue distribution to frame the room:

- ~36 million businesses in the U.S.
- Only ~5% ever hit $1M/year revenue.
- $1M–$3M: ~1.2 million businesses (3.3%). $3M–$10M: ~600K (1.7%). Only **1.2% ever break $10M**. Then 0.4% above that.
- "The jumps [are] really little… very, very difficult."
- This room: ~80% above $1M; 58% at $1–5M; 21% at $5–15M; a few 9-figure companies. Average attendee revenue $5.1M, median $2.5M ("the big dogs push it down" — he means pull the median down relative to the mean). Room is ~16x the U.S. average, and ~9.5x the average on the $10M+ threshold.

**Takeaway embedded in the client shout-outs:** when he asked growth-story attendees "what's the biggest thing you did?", the answer over and over was **"Charge more money"** / raise prices. ("Did you raise prices too?" — "Every time we're here.") Pricing is the recurring first lever in this community.

### 2. The mentor question and belief constraints

Origin story: Alex and Leila were stuck at $30M/year for two years. What unstuck them was not a tactic but a new mentor (their "first billionaire friend") who changed what they believed. The speaker's framing:

- "They did not have a business constraint. They had a **belief constraint**. They believed the wrong things. Once they could see the world more clearly, they took off faster than before."
- The best question to ask a mentor (verbatim): **"What do you, someone ahead of me, see in me that I believe about the world that's not true?"**
- Hormozi wisdom: "If someone is ahead of me in the game of business, I can and should learn from them."
- Their trajectory once this compounded: $0 → $30M in 20 months at Gym Launch; $250M in 24 months at Acquisition.com; then "$106 million in a weekend." The point: velocity keeps increasing when beliefs update faster.

### 3. Dumb / Smart / Genius — intelligence as rate of behavior change

Definition-obsessed framework (he cites Socrates: "the beginning of wisdom is the definition of terms"; and Confucius: "if names are not correct, what is said does not accord with the truth of things"):

- **Learning** = "same condition, changed behavior." If the same thing happens to you and you respond differently, you learned. "If you take a bunch of notes and change nothing, have you learned? No. Not allowed to do that."
- **Intelligence** = your **rate of learning** — how fast you change behavior. Explicitly not IQ; it is operationalizable.
- **Dumb people** make the same mistakes again and again. **Smart people** make a mistake once, learn, change — growth in steps (grow, plateau, grow, plateau). **Genius people** learn from the mistakes of *others* — a compounding learning curve. "Being a genius is not about IQ… It's a behavior, so you can decide to be a genius literally right now."
- Requires "a tremendous amount of humility and super, super low, if not zero, ego." Second-favorite quote: **"Learn the lessons and skip the scars."**
- The challenge to the room: "Be the most intelligent person you know — meaning move faster than everybody in your life… just change behavior faster." Safety valve: "if it doesn't work, you can always go back."

### 4. Flywheels — linear vs. compounding effort

The billionaire mentor's core teaching, per the speaker: build a **flywheel**, defined as "a compounding system."

- **Compounding** = "the output of effort gets reused again as input" (jokingly, "a self-licking ice cream cone").
- **Linear**: 1 unit of effort → 1 unit of result. 1 unit/day for 100 days = 100 units, period.
- **Compounding**: 1 unit of effort → 5, 10, 20, 50, 100 units via leverage. Same 100 days of 1-unit effort = "five hundred to two thousand plus units of results."
- Design your marketing so outputs (content, recordings, testimonials, data) feed back in as inputs.

### 5. Radical change #1 — "Social media is dead"; interest media is alive

- "It's no longer about followers. It's about interest. The algorithm fundamentally does not care about how many followers you have, and only cares about people interested in the content you post."
- Live proof from the room: one attendee had a 200K+ video (600K views total) with **1,000 followers**; another had **4M views on TikTok with 25,000 followers**. "You could have zero followers today and go viral."
- Case study: a client making gym equipment ("metal grinding gears… boring") with **865 followers** posts **11-minute-long TikToks** about gym equipment. Alex Hormozi — who owns a 15,000 sq ft commercial gym and "all he thinks about is buying equipment" — is served that content because his watch history says so. Ideal buyer reached with zero targeting and zero audience.
- The core line: **"Content is the targeting."** ("The content you post is the targeting. You don't need any followers, literally.")
- Consumer-side confirmation: everyone knows the "if I watch too long I'm gonna see more of it, so scroll faster" instinct — the algorithm reads dwell/interest, and you already behave as if that's true.
- Corollary on vanity metrics: "You could have two hundred and fifty views on the video — if it's super, super niche, the right people will only see it. And we want that to be the case." Low views on hyper-specific content can be a *feature*.
- Urgency: "This is not normal and this will not last forever… there are cycles of algorithms and interest. In this moment right now, you can get literally millions of free eyeballs. We want to really maximize this while this window's open."

### 6. Radical change #2 — Meta's Andromeda update: "the creative IS the targeting"

- Timing: "around October twenty twenty-five, the Andromeda update came out on Meta." Room poll: most felt it was *hurting* their business; his position: "it's the best f***ing thing ever… the absolute cheat code of cheat codes in marketing."
- What changed: before, Meta let you steer with **interests, demographics, lookalikes** — "this matters less and less every day." Now delivery is driven by **your ad creative** plus the actions users take on and off platform. "Meta knows more about the people you're trying to target than anyone… thousands of data points… **the ad creative matters more than anything else**… which means **the creative for your paid ads is also the target**."
- Under-delivery reframed: "Does anybody spend a lot on Meta… and notice that it doesn't even spend the budget sometimes? …That's good. It's literally not wasting your money. It doesn't want you to show ads that are not high quality or very relevant… it's protecting your budget for you."
- **The prescription: volume and quality of creative.** "To win today, you need way more ad ideas, way more creative angles, and way better videos and images. The quality has to go up. You need way more volume."
- Benchmarks from the room and from Acquisition.com:
  - Attendee (Carter, $50K+/mo spender): makes **30–40 creatives per week**.
  - Acquisition.com accounts: average **~50 unique creatives live** at any time, launching **~20 new per week** — "we're probably going to double or triple that literally."
  - Alex's Meta ad library that morning: **~900 active creatives**.
- The creative formats in that 900-ad library (study "the variety and specificity"):
  1. **Q&A clips** — real calls with clients/prospects ("I sell cleaning services… I sell marketing services… I sell real estate coaching"). Critically: **"They say what they do in the first three seconds. Why? The algorithm's like, oh — real estate coaching. Send to people that need that."** Self-identification in the opening seconds is a targeting signal.
  2. **Niche call-outs** — "If you own a jewelry brand and you want a scaling roadmap… if you own a metal fabrication business… if you own an insurance company…" Very specific verbal call-outs, with "different wrappers [on] the same stuff."
  3. **Client testimonials** — they collect "probably a hundred or so a week, and we run all of them. And some of them win; some don't." Key admission: **"Do you know how we can predict which ones win and don't win? Me neither — there is no way to tell… Every time I'm like 'this is the winner,' it bombs… You just do a lot and the algorithm decides for you."** Do not pre-filter creative by taste; ship everything and let delivery sort it.
  4. **Lead magnets.**
  5. **AI-generated ads** — he played an AI avatar clip of "Alex" that fooled the room ("That's AI… it is getting better every day… so we have to lean into it").

### 7. Radical change #3 — AI gives infinite scale (Threads case study)

- Baseline: Alex was essentially inactive on Threads ("I don't want another thing to do").
- Progression: one team member manually posting **2x/day** → a little growth. Manual **5x/day** → a little growth. Then **24 posts/day via AI workflows** (one per hour) sustained for 4–5 months.
- Result: **+903,000 net new followers, "and there was no human touching it."** The workflow was "built one time to scrape his tweets, repost it, schedule it, test it. That's it. So you can build this once and it just runs. Robots are great."
- Lesson: the marginal cost of distribution is collapsing; the leverage is in building the workflow once, not in per-post labor.

### 8. Theory of Constraints — the diagnostic lens

- **Constraint is singular.** "Priority only became pluralized in like the fifties or sixties… priority means one." Roadblock, not roadblocks.
- Common misconception corrected: the constraint is not just "the thing that stops you from growing." **"It's the one thing that if you just did this, everything else is irrelevant."** Even a steadily growing business has one move that unlocks hypergrowth. "You have many, many problems with one constraint" — solve it and "all the other problems go away. You'll have new problems later, I promise."
- **The AI 2x2** (only four ways to use AI in business):
  | | Right AI tool | Wrong AI tool |
  |---|---|---|
  | **Right problem (the constraint)** | Growth | "Feels like failing, kind of sucks" |
  | **Wrong problem** | "This one's insidious — it's the one that's going to really burn your money" (busy all month, bank account unchanged) | "Just light money on fire" |
  - Litmus test he ran on the room: "I use AI every day" is meaningless unless the answer to "is the business growing a lot since?" is yes. "If you're working all the hours… and your business is not growing enough, you're doing the wrong s***. We just need to find the right thing to attack."
- **Worked example 1 — e-commerce funnel (the cartoon Alex-cutout factory):** 3 steps: opt-in → add-to-cart → checkout. Current rates: 75% opt-in, 50% add-to-cart, 8% checkout. 1,000 clicks → 750 opt-ins → 375 add-to-carts → **30 checkouts**. You can bump exactly one step by 5 points:
  - Opt-in 75→80%: a **6% relative lift** in sales.
  - Add-to-cart 50→55%: a **10% relative lift**.
  - Checkout 8→13%: a **63% relative lift**.
  - Same effort, same resources — pick the step with the **biggest lift relative to its starting point**. "If I improve any of them, I make more money, but only one has the highest returns. We have to diagnose our marketing funnel better."
- **Worked example 2 — coaching/call funnel** (directly applicable to any consult-based business): **booking rate 35%, show rate 25%, close rate 70%.** The constraint is obviously show rate — "If I lift this, way more sales."

### 9. AI plays per funnel stage (from the live brainstorm)

**To fix show rate:**
- **Pain-based personalized reminders** (audience suggestion he upgraded): "Remind them of their **pain** to get them to show up. Don't remind them of the appointment. Remind them of the pain they have and the problem they want to solve. Way different than 'reminder at three o'clock today.' [Instead:] 'Reminder: we're going to solve a big, giant pain in your life at three o'clock today.'"
- **Matched proof**: scrape/collect the prospect's details with AI and match them to "your hopefully incredible library of detailed testimonials" — show them "someone just like them" between booking and showing.
- **Re-script the pre-show VSL from buyer language**: analyze recordings of people who actually bought and use their exact words in the video prospects watch between booking and the call. Acquisition.com proof point: **"We took eleven thousand sales recordings… analyzed all of the key pain points… changed our front-end marketing and had like a forty percent lift."** ("There's no way one person that runs a sales team could know all that information" — but AI can.)

**To fix close rate:**
- **AI-personalized follow-ups** that reinstate the actual sales conversation: "Hey, remember we talked about this?"
- **The client-memory podcast**: for client-services businesses — "write down everything about the client… take all the information about a client and then create a podcast, just for you, with AI voices, and just listen to it" before you see them again. Produces the "Wow, you remembered me" effect that Acquisition.com engineers deliberately.
- **"Killing zombies"** — objection front-loading: "Zombies are things that come up at the end of the sale when you ask for money and they eat your money. You want to kill zombies before they show up." Use AI to find every objection across your closed-call transcripts, then pull them into the *front* of the script. Worked script for "I gotta think about it":
  > "Hey, before we get started — we only have one rule about this program, which is **we decide today**. And the reason is we track the data: people that make a decision today versus people that decide later… the people that decided later still get results, but not the best results. Why do you think that is?"
  Then make the *prospect* answer ("they probably just weren't committed") — "See how I put the pressure on him? …You label the thing that you don't want them to say later. But I wouldn't know that unless I actually analyzed the calls."

**To fix booking rate:**
- **Improve the offer** (nod to $100M Offers — his joke: "There's a book about it by Greg Norman").
- **AI-powered split testing at volume**: "Imagine I split test the headline and the CTA and the color of the button and the first five seconds of the VSL… ten different ways… If you get one, two, three percent more throughput, your business grows by a lot."
- **Pull buyer verbiage into the pre-booking VSL/page** — same transcript-mining idea applied before booking.

### 10. Meta-lessons on execution (closing)

- Don't try to write everything down. At every break, "just write down **the one thing you are going to do** — the behavior you are going to do." 5–7 committed behavior changes across the workshop "is more than enough."
- "**We need to be reminded more than we need to be taught.**" (His all-time quote.)
- Team management statistic: **"You have to tell your team seven times the same thing for them to believe it's actually important."** Repetition is the job.
- Urgency framing: "Those who take action now in this season will have an unfair advantage over everybody else."
